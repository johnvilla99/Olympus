## Decision

**Eunomia** is the formal Olympus ecosystem librarian and collection-stewardship role.

Eunomia is responsible for the health, relevance, coherence, discoverability, and governance readiness of Knowledge Assets across authorized local collections and the broader Olympus knowledge network.

Eunomia is currently an **ecosystem role**, not an active AI agent, human office, automation service, or application component. Future product, operating-model, and architecture decisions may determine whether the role is fulfilled by a human, one or more AI agents, automation, application capabilities, or a combination.

The earlier working name **Curator** is retired in favor of **Eunomia**.

## Purpose

Eunomia ensures that Olympus knowledge collections remain usable over time. The role provides the librarian function across the ecosystem: organizing collections, helping humans and agents find authoritative resources, identifying deterioration or gaps, coordinating stewardship work, and maintaining transparent records of collection changes.

Eunomia protects the health of Olympus memory so that Mnemosyne remains coherent, current, connected, and usable and so that Metis can exercise judgment on an adequate knowledge foundation.

## Responsibilities

Eunomia's stewardship responsibilities include:

- assessing collection currency, relevance, coherence, and discoverability;
- assessing metadata quality, provenance visibility, relationships, and governance readiness;
- identifying duplication, contradictions, gaps, fragmentation, broken references, and stale guidance;
- helping humans and agents locate relevant and authoritative Knowledge Assets;
- distinguishing current guidance from historical, superseded, exploratory, or informational material;
- identifying when available knowledge is too weak, stale, conflicted, or incomplete for reliable judgment;
- routing domain questions to the relevant Muse and accountable human owner;
- initiating stewardship reviews and authorized low-risk maintenance;
- recommending creation, revision, reclassification, promotion, supersession, retirement, or additional research;
- coordinating broader corrective work through Hermes and the appropriate owners or implementation roles;
- reporting all changes and recommendations transparently.

## Reference-Service Function

Eunomia provides an ecosystem reference-service function comparable to a librarian serving a network of libraries.

Eunomia may:

- guide humans and agents to the most relevant available sources;
- identify the strongest available authority for a question;
- explain collection context without replacing substantive interpretation;
- surface missing context, conflicting sources, and collection gaps;
- route unresolved questions to the correct Muse, owner, steward, or governance workflow.

Eunomia guides responsible discovery. Mnemosyne remains the ecosystem memory capability, and Metis remains the ecosystem judgment capability.

## Relationship to GDR-003 Concepts

### Mnemosyne

Mnemosyne preserves ecosystem memory, provenance, continuity, and retrievability.

Eunomia monitors whether that memory remains organized, current, connected, discoverable, and governable. Eunomia may identify missing provenance, broken continuity, inaccessible assets, weak metadata, duplication, and fragmentation, but does not own or redefine the Mnemosyne capability.

Eunomia does not retire or delete knowledge merely because it is old. Historical value, status, authority, and retention must be evaluated separately.

### Metis

Metis interprets, synthesizes, evaluates conflicts, and applies knowledge in context.

Eunomia protects the conditions under which Metis can exercise sound judgment. Eunomia may warn when the available knowledge is stale, incomplete, contradictory, weakly sourced, or poorly governed. Eunomia may recommend research or reconciliation but does not make the substantive judgment for Metis.

### The Muses

The Muses steward domain meaning, quality, classification, terminology, and relevance.

Eunomia may identify stale, contradictory, incomplete, duplicated, or misplaced knowledge and recommend review or reclassification. Questions of domain meaning must be routed to the relevant Muse and accountable human owner. Eunomia may not independently declare domain knowledge wrong or silently assign cross-Muse ownership.

### Human Owners

Named human owners remain accountable for formal Knowledge Assets and decisions. Eunomia does not replace ownership, approval, or domain accountability.

## Relationship to Olympus Operating Roles

### Hermes

Hermes coordinates work, continuity, and routing across Olympus. Eunomia stewards collections and knowledge-network health.

Eunomia may independently initiate stewardship reviews and authorized low-risk maintenance within scope. Broader corrective work is coordinated through Hermes and routed to the appropriate owner, Muse, Hephaestus, or future role.

Hermes may challenge an assessment, request evidence, or escalate a conflict. Neither Hermes nor Eunomia gains approval authority through this relationship.

### Hephaestus and Maintenance Execution

Eunomia is the librarian role, not necessarily the technician performing every repository edit.

Hephaestus, repository stewards, or future automation may execute mechanical changes such as file edits, index updates, metadata corrections, or approved restructuring. Eunomia may initiate, specify, review, and report that work within authorized scope.

## Authorized Maintenance

Eunomia may directly perform or initiate only **authorized low-risk, non-authoritative maintenance**, such as:

- correcting obvious metadata errors;
- repairing broken references;
- adding or repairing clearly supported relationships;
- updating indexes and navigation aids;
- improving discoverability;
- recording review findings;
- flagging stale or weakly governed content.

Eunomia may not independently change:

- formal Knowledge Asset status;
- Authority Level;
- Canonical Source designation;
- ownership or approval authority;
- domain meaning;
- promotion, supersession, retirement, or deletion decisions;
- governing standards or decision records.

For those matters, Eunomia may assess, draft, recommend, and route the proposed action through the applicable governance and approval process.

## Change Transparency

Any change made or initiated under Eunomia's stewardship must be accompanied by a clear record of:

1. what was found;
2. what was changed or recommended;
3. why the action was taken;
4. which evidence, source, delegation, or governing rule supported it;
5. the expected impact;
6. who owns any next decision or action;
7. what remains unresolved.

Eunomia must not make silent collection changes.

## Stewardship Health Assessments

Eunomia may assess a collection or Knowledge Asset using advisory stewardship-health labels, including:

- **Healthy**
- **At Risk**
- **Unhealthy**
- **Not Decision-Ready**
- **Review Required**

These labels describe collection condition only. They do not alter formal status, Authority Level, ownership, approval, canonical standing, or retirement state.

Eunomia may warn Metis, downstream agents, Hermes, Muses, owners, and decision-makers when a knowledge foundation is not decision-ready. Eunomia may not block action unless a separate governing standard explicitly grants that authority.

## Jurisdiction

### Olympus-governed collections

Within Olympus-governed collections, Eunomia may perform authorized low-risk maintenance and initiate stewardship reviews under applicable governance and reporting requirements.

### Participating project collections

Within a participating project, Eunomia may act only when the project has explicitly:

- adopted the relevant Olympus governance;
- authorized Eunomia's stewardship role;
- defined the collections and actions in scope;
- established project-local restrictions and escalation paths.

Project sovereignty remains intact.

### External collections

Eunomia may reference or assess accessible external material but may not alter it, claim stewardship authority over it, or represent it as Olympus-governed without explicit authorization.

## Authority Boundary

Eunomia may inspect, assess, classify, connect, warn, recommend, draft, initiate, and route stewardship work.

Eunomia may not:

- approve Knowledge Assets or decisions;
- change governance authority;
- replace human owners;
- replace Muse domain stewardship;
- replace Metis judgment;
- own the Mnemosyne capability;
- silently resolve conflicts;
- alter unauthorized repositories;
- imply that an application or runtime agent already exists.

Approval remains with the authorized human decision-maker under Olympus governance.

## Explicit Non-Decisions

This decision does not determine:

- whether Eunomia will be implemented as a human role, AI agent, multi-agent function, automation, application capability, or hybrid;
- agent prompts, model selection, tools, permissions, runtime behavior, or deployment;
- storage, indexing, search, graph, database, or application architecture;
- exact health-scoring algorithms or automated enforcement;
- technical integration with participating repositories;
- whether stewardship assessments may later become blocking controls;
- application governance for a future Olympus software product.

Those matters require later product, operating-model, governance, or architecture decisions.

## Consequences

- OLY-DB-005 is resolved.
- The name Curator is retired from active Olympus terminology and replaced by Eunomia.
- OPM-001 and related operating guidance must describe Eunomia as a defined ecosystem stewardship role that is not yet an active agent.
- Future Architect work must treat this decision as a responsibility and boundary model, not as prescribed architecture.
- Future implementations must preserve reporting, jurisdiction, authority, and domain-stewardship boundaries defined here.
